Read the license, not just the leaderboard.

Historically, many forecasting models were available under permissive licenses. Libraries such as StatsForecast (Apache 2.0) and pretrained models such as Amazon Chronos (Apache 2.0) made it relatively straightforward to experiment, self-host, and deploy forecasting solutions commercially.

Today, the picture is becoming more nuanced:

  • Google TimesFM 3.0: Pretrained weights are restricted to non-commercial, non-production use.
  • Salesforce Moirai: Published pretrained weights use CC-BY-NC 4.0, which restricts commercial use.
  • Mistral Timestral: the famous recent model's weights use a modified MIT license that requires companies exceeding $20M in monthly revenue to obtain a commercial license or use Mistral's hosted services.

It is not surprising: these are foundation models requiring substantial investment in data, research, and pretraining infrastructure. Monetizing that investment is entirely understandable.

But for enterprises, model selection is no longer just about accuracy and inference costs. Licensing, total cost of ownership, deployment flexibility, and vendor dependency matter just as much.

It brings an important consideration for enterprise users: in this reality, it's becoming essential to evaluate model weights separately from code, check commercial terms early, and keep your forecasting architecture model-agnostic.

Frontier models for frontier problems.

I see an interesting shift in the industry: moving from relying almost exclusively on frontier models towards architectures that combine proprietary frontier models with capable open-weight alternatives (such as GLM), or SLMs.

I believe this shift is driven by two important considerations:

1. Cost efficiency through intelligent model routing

Not every task requires a frontier model. Classification, information extraction, summarization and many other relatively well-defined tasks can often be handled by smaller, cheaper open-weight models. A model routing layer can direct requests to the most appropriate model based on task complexity, performance requirements and cost.

The economics are particularly interesting when open models can run on dedicated infrastructure, either on-premises or in the cloud. Of course, the actual business case needs to account for GPU investment, utilization, infrastructure and operational costs.

2. Availability and resilience through intelligent load balancing and failover

A model gateway can also become an important part of the enterprise AI infrastructure strategy. Imagine prioritizing on-premises GPU capacity, bursting to cloud infrastructure during demand peaks, and falling back to alternative model providers when an API becomes unavailable.

This creates flexibility in managing capacity, cost and availability. There is, however, an important nuance: model failover is not always transparent. Switching between instances of the same model is relatively straightforward. Switching from a frontier model to an open-weight alternative can change output quality and behavior.

Fallback strategies therefore need to be defined and tested at the use-case level, with clear quality thresholds and escalation paths.

I see model routing and orchestration becoming increasingly important building blocks of enterprise AI architecture. However, don't get me wrong: I don't think the objective is to replace frontier models with open models. It's to stop paying frontier-model prices for tasks that don't need frontier-level capabilities, while building systems that remain available when individual models or providers fail.

By the way, it will be interesting to see what this shift means for the frontier labs from a revenue perspective.

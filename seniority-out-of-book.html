Seniority starts when you're out of book.

One thing I believe about engineering (data science and AI engineering included) seniority:

The more senior an engineer becomes, the less they should be defined by the problems they know how to solve, and the more by their ability to solve problems they haven't encountered before.

Early in your career, building deep expertise in a particular technology, domain or problem space is essential (or at least it was in the pre-AI era). You learn the tools, master the patterns and become really good at solving a specific class of problems.

But at some point, seniority should mean something different: a senior engineer shouldn't just be someone who knows more technologies or has seen more implementations. They should be someone who can approach an unfamiliar problem, break it down, identify the right questions, find the necessary expertise, and navigate their way towards a solution.

This is what I would call developing a problem-solving muscle. Like any muscle, it takes exercise. It grows by tackling unfamiliar challenges, stepping outside your comfort zone, experimenting, making mistakes and learning how to navigate ambiguity.

Specialization gives you depth. Problem-solving gives you range. And at the highest levels of engineering, especially in the AI era, the latter becomes increasingly important.
